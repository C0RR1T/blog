# Tour du Mont Blanc

I recently hiked the Tour du Mont Blanc with a friend of mine, and I must say, it was one of the most beautiful hikes I've done.
I cannot recommend this hike enough, and so I thought I'll write a little blog post to help you plan your own TMB hike!

As a heads-up, this was my first hut-to-hut hike and only my second multi-day hike after the West Highland Way (check out my other blog post for that)
Also, when I planned my TMB I heavily relied on [this blog bost from Well and Good Travel](https://wellandgoodtravel.com/tour-du-mont-blanc-itinerary/).
I will shorten the name to WAGT for the rest of this blog, because it's alot to type and I'm lazy (I am a programmer after all)

## Planning

My friend and I hiked the TMB at the end of August, which I recommend you do as well. The weather was still pretty warm (sometimes to hot for my liking)
and it rained for 1 day on the entire 11 days (yes, we hiked it in 11 days, more on that later).

We also booked the refuges on our own, just like WAGT did. I recommend you do the same, as it's not that hard to figure out and it's really expensive
otherwise.
We started booking in September, and by then some refuges had already been fully booked, so if you can, I'll recommend booking it even earlier than that. Or just bring a tent :)

## Day 1: Trient to Argèntiere

We started our hike in Trient, which would normally be the second-to-last day. We're swiss and wanted to have a "quick" travel time, so we decided to stay in Martigny, and then go up to Trient with the Bus.
I personally liked Martigny, I wouldn't call it the prettiest city in the world, but it has everything you need.
I would definetly travel to Chamonix for the next time, just because I liked it so much.
If you can, I'd definetly would follow the advice of WAGT to stay in Chamonix before and after your trip.

On our first day, we hiked into France via Col de Balme. The first ascent was pretty steep, but totally doable.
Once you arrived at the refuge that is there, you will be amazed on how beautiful everything is.
You will also see the Mont Blanc massive for the first time (if the weather allows it...)!

Once you passed the refuge, you'll notice that you're actually in a ski resort. This was a first for me,
I found it really interesting how steep the slopes actually are.
We went up to the Aigullette des Posettes. It also had great views, but the path there was pretty technical.
Nothing impossible, but you definetly had to watch your steps.

The descent into Tre Le Champ / Argèntiere was pretty brutal and I was definetly glad that I brought hiking poles with me.

## Night 1: Gîte Le Moulin

Once we were in Argèntiere (or Montroc, to be specific), we checked into our first accomodation. I can definetly recommend this refuge!
The rooms were for 4 people, and everything was clean. The hosts were really friendly and the food was great.

Since it was like 14:00 when we arrived, we decided to go to Argèntiere via bus. You can just tap in with card when you're going on the bus.
There is a small ice cream stand at the town square. Can definetly recommend that one.

## Day 2: Argèntiere to Les Houches

Today was our day for the somewhat famous ladder section. Funnily enough, at dinner we spoke to a guy which had this stage left to go.
I was talking with him about the stage and he always seemed to ignore my comments about the ladder section. Turned out he didn't even
know that the ladder section was a thing, and probably thought I misspoke (since english isn't my first language, and I have a pretty big accent). He said he would've skipped it if he knew.

To be perfectly honest, If it was my last day I probably would've skipped it, but as this was only our second day we kinda had and wanted to do it.
I have a bit of a fear of heights, but I can assure you it's easily doable. Just take your time and just look at the ladder or at your feet.
In the end I didn't have any problems, but it was kinda exhausting nonetheless.

Once you finished the ladder section, you could go either up to Lac Blanc or directly to the cable car. We did the Lac Blanc variant because WAGT recommended it in her blog.
We didn't really like it to be honest. In her blog she said "a bit of elevation gain", which turned out to be the understatement of the year.
I think it's comparable to the ladder section again. At Lac Blanc there is a hut where you can eat some tasty tarts, but other than that it's not that exciting.
Also there were a lot of day hikers there, which made the ascent and descent pretty slow. If you have enough energy, I think it's pretty doable, but don't expect anything crazy.

After Lac Blanc, we went to the La Flégère cable car. You can also continue to the Planpraz cable car if you want to, but we decided
we had enough hiking for the day, and we wanted to explore Chamonix a bit.

I really liked Chamonix, I definetly have to go back there in winter. It's a beatuiful little city and you can kinda feel the sportive vibe,
it's hard to explain but I think you'll get what I mean once you get there.

## Night 2: Gîte Michel Fagot

This refuge is in Les Houches, just a bus ride away from Chamonix. I can also recommend this refuge, the food was pretty tasty and the rooms and shower were really nice.
You also have a sink and washing line available to wash your clothes. Just note that they only take cash.
There is an ATM nearby, but apparently the fees are pretty bad, I'd recommend getting cash in Chamonix.
